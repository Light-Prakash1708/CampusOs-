# CampusOS customer discovery interview guide (CAMPUSOS-002)

**Purpose.** Find out whether a college will **pay** for CampusOS, not whether people find it interesting. Every interview tries to fill in this equation:

> **Pain × Frequency × Existing workaround × Budget × Decision-maker × Measurable outcome = Potential willingness to pay**

## Ground rules

- **Past behaviour, not hypotheticals.** Ask "Tell me about the last time…", not "Would you use…?".
- **Show the product last.** The first 70% of the interview is about their world.
- **Don't pitch.** Don't correct them, and don't argue.
- **Record only what they said.** Notes are anonymised: role, institution type and size band. No names or phone numbers go in the repository.
- **Ask for consent** before recording audio. Delete audio after synthesis.
- **Compliments are noise.** Commitments are signal: time, data, a pilot, an introduction, money.
- **Target:** at least 10 principal/IQAC interviews, 30 student interviews, 5 faculty interviews and 3 placement-cell interviews.

---

## 1. Principal / Director / Management (economic buyer)

**Opening**

1. Tell me about your college: students, programmes, how long you've run it, and your NAAC status and cycle.
2. What are the top three things that keep you busy in a typical week?
3. What problem, if it disappeared tomorrow, would make the biggest difference?

**Existing ERP and tools**

4. Which software do you use today (ERP, LMS, attendance, placement, communication)? Who chose it, and when?
5. What do you pay for it? How is it priced: per student, flat fee, or modules?
6. What does it do well? What do staff or students complain about?
7. Which parts of it do students actually use?
8. Have you ever switched software? What triggered the switch, and how long did it take?

**Communication**

9. When the college must tell every student something important (exam schedule, fee deadline, holiday), how does it happen today?
10. Tell me about the last time a student said "nobody told me". What happened next?
11. How do you know a notice reached every student? Does anyone check?
12. How many WhatsApp groups does the college rely on? Who manages them?

**Grievances**

13. How do students raise complaints today? Is there an SGRC and an ombudsperson?
14. How many grievances were raised last semester? How long did they take to resolve? Where is that recorded?
15. Has a grievance ever escalated to the university, UGC, a court or the media? What did that cost you?

**Accreditation**

16. How was evidence for your last NAAC/NBA/NIRF submission gathered? Who did it, and how many person-weeks did it take?
17. Which criteria or data were hardest to produce?
18. Have you paid consultants or software for accreditation? How much?

**Budget and decision**

19. Who else would be involved in deciding on a new tool (trust, IQAC, IT, HODs)?
20. When is your budget planned? Is there a line item for software or student services?
21. What would a new tool need to show within one semester for you to keep it?

**Product reaction** (show the demo only now)

22. What, if anything, here would you use first? What would you ignore?
23. What would stop you from rolling this out to all students?
24. "Works alongside your ERP": is that a plus, a minus, or irrelevant to you?

**Pricing** (see §8)

**Pilot commitment** (see §9)

## 2. IQAC coordinator (champion)

1. Walk me through your last accreditation cycle from start to finish.
2. Where does the evidence live today (Excel, Google Drive, ERP reports, paper)?
3. Which data do departments send you late or incomplete?
4. Show me a report you had to build by hand. How long did it take?
5. How do you currently evidence student support: mentoring, grievances, notices, feedback?
6. How do you collect student feedback or satisfaction data today? What response rates do you get?
7. If one export gave you notice-acknowledgement rates, grievance resolution times and attendance summaries per term, what would you do with it? What else would it need?
8. Who would you have to convince to try it?

## 3. Faculty

1. Walk me through yesterday: which tools did you open, and for what?
2. How do you mark attendance today? How long does it take per class? Where does it go?
3. How do you reach your students about an assignment or a class change?
4. Tell me about the last time students missed something you announced.
5. What happens when a student's attendance is getting low? Who notices, and when?
6. What would make you refuse a new app?
7. (Demo) Is posting here and seeing who acknowledged more or less work than what you do now?

## 4. Students

1. How many WhatsApp groups are you in for college? How many do you actually read?
2. Tell me about the last time you missed something important from college. What happened?
3. How do you know your attendance percentage today? When did you last check?
4. Have you or a friend ever been close to a shortage or detention? What did you do?
5. How do you find internships, hackathons and scholarships?
6. How do you decide what skills to learn?
7. Have you ever had a complaint about college? Did you raise it? Why or why not?
8. What apps do you use daily for college? Which would you delete?
9. (Demo: attendance planner) Would you use this? How often? Would you share it with classmates?
10. What would make you distrust an app your college asks you to use?
11. Would you use a personal version even if your college didn't adopt it?

## 5. Placement cell / Training & Placement Officer

1. How do you track which students are placement-ready?
2. Which tools do you use (Superset, Unstop, Excel, others)? What do you pay?
3. How do you share opportunities with students? How do you know who saw them?
4. What data does management ask you for? How long does it take to prepare?
5. (Demo: cohort readiness, opportunities) Is this useful, redundant, or in conflict with your current tool?

## 6. Existing ERP deep-dive (any admin role)

1. Which ERP modules are switched on, and which are actually used?
2. Does your ERP have a student app, messaging, a grievance module or mentoring? **Do students use them? What is the evidence?**
3. Can you export student lists, sections, timetables and attendance to CSV? How often?
4. What would the ERP vendor charge to add what you need?
5. Would your ERP vendor or IT team object to a tool alongside it?

## 7. Topic modules (use as relevant)

| Topic | Key questions |
|---|---|
| **WhatsApp** | Who creates the groups? What happens when admins leave? Have notices ever been lost? Privacy concerns (phone numbers exposed)? |
| **Attendance** | What threshold applies? How are shortage lists produced and communicated? When do students first learn they are short? |
| **Grievances** | Which categories are most common? Anonymous complaints? Who sits on the SGRC? Is the 15-working-day report tracked? |
| **NAAC evidence** | Which criteria are hardest? Do you use consultants? When is your next cycle? |

## 8. Pricing validation

Ask these **after** the pain is established, never first.

1. What do you pay per year for your ERP? For placement tools? For SMS/WhatsApp messaging?
2. **Van Westendorp**, per student per year:
   - At what price would this be so cheap you'd doubt its quality?
   - At what price is it a bargain?
   - At what price does it start to feel expensive but still worth considering?
   - At what price is it too expensive to consider?
3. Would you prefer a per-student price, a flat fee, or a module-based price? Why?
4. Who signs a purchase of this size? Does it need a tender or committee approval?

**Record the answers against the price hypotheses without steering:**

- **Starter:** ₹150–300 per student per year.
- **Professional:** ₹300–600 per student per year.

These are **hypotheses**, not prices.

## 9. Pilot commitment

The strongest signal: ask for something concrete, in this order.

1. "Can we run a one-semester pilot with one department or year?"
2. "Can you share a CSV of that department's students and sections?"
3. "Who would be our day-to-day contact?"
4. "If the pilot shows [the outcome they named in Q21], would you pay [the price they named] next semester?"
5. "Who else, in this college or another, should I talk to?"

**Record exactly** what was agreed, with a date. "Sounds interesting" is recorded as **no commitment**.

## Product-market-fit signals to listen for

- "Can we use this next semester?"
- "Can you integrate this with our ERP?"
- "Can you add our other college?"
- "Can our IQAC use this?"
- "Can we have WhatsApp?"
- "Can you give this to all departments?"
